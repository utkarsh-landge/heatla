# Supporting literature for the four added ACS features

Selected by the team on 2026-11-08. These papers motivate feature selection; no study data, fitted coefficients or risk scores are imported into HEATLA.

## Feature-to-evidence mapping

| HEATLA feature | Main reference | Supported rationale | Boundary of the evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Construction-industry employment share | Riley et al. (2018) | LA ZIP-level construction employment was associated with heat-related ED visits. | Community association, not a causal coefficient for HEATLA; construction is not all outdoor work. |
| Households without a vehicle | Derakhshan et al. (2024) | Car access was examined against excess ER outcomes and differed between high- and low-burden tracts. | Not a direct measure of travel time; access barriers may also affect whether illness appears in ER records. |
| Uninsured population share | Hess et al. (2014); Derakhshan et al. (2024) | Insurance status is relevant to heat-illness ED burden and healthcare utilization. | Patient-level findings do not establish a monotonic ZIP-level effect; county-level associations can differ in direction. |
| Renter-occupied housing units | Scott et al. (2025); Derakhshan et al. (2024) | Renting can indicate unequal residential cooling resources and is included in vulnerability research. | AC-access evidence is indirect, not proof of higher ER rates or that every renter lacks cooling. |

## Verification status

Riley: methods and results checked, especially ACS industry definition and Table 3. Derakhshan: methods, Tables 1–2, section 3.5 and discussion checked. Its 2009–2018 daily excess-rate outcome differs from HEATLA's category-4 label. Hess: methods, Table 2 and conclusion checked; the uninsured group had the highest treat-and-release population rate, which is not a claim that it led every disposition category.

Scott: publisher abstract, highlights and bibliographic record checked. The accessible abstract reports higher odds of lacking AC among renter-occupants using the 2021 metropolitan American Housing Survey. Full-text methods, coefficients and sensitivity analyses have **not** been reviewed. The report uses only this abstract-supported, indirect rationale. Full-text review remains an open verification item, not a reason to claim a confirmed ER effect.
